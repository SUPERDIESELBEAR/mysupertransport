## 2. Shape used, and why it deviates from nullable → backfill → NOT NULL

Seven tables were empty and took the standard route (nullable → NOT NULL, no default).

Three were populated — `rods_days` (2), `rods_correction_requests` (3),
`blank_log_acknowledgments` (1) — and took the constant-`DEFAULT`-then-`DROP DEFAULT`
route. A backfill `UPDATE` on these rows would fire the RODS lock, continuity and
append-only triggers, i.e. an `UPDATE` against a certified federal record. The triggers
were **not** suspended. This is the second use of the constant-default route recorded
under the first-instance override rule.

Every populated row was checked against the company derived from its own operator BEFORE
the migration: `rods_days` 2/2, `rods_correction_requests` 3/3,
`blank_log_acknowledgments` 1/1, all one company
`6b54d0e6-8743-4284-b55b-8cd094b093dd`.

All ten received: `company_id` NOT NULL, no default, FK to `carrier_profile`
`ON DELETE RESTRICT`, a company index, and the `aa_stamp_tenant_company_id` insert stamp.
No unique index needed re-scoping — the existing keys are `(operator_id, …)` or global
idempotency keys.

## 5. Security finding `has_role_not_company_scoped` — fixed and demonstrated

`public.has_role` and `public.is_staff` now require
`ur.company_id = public.current_company_id()`. Retained escape:
`current_company_id() IS NULL`, for unauthenticated and service-role contexts, which hold
no `user_roles` rows and bypass RLS anyway. Both are SECURITY DEFINER with
`search_path = public, extensions`.

Genuine demonstration, with both arms, using scratch data since only one carrier exists:

1. Scratch carrier `11111111-…` inserted (service-role stamp path), and a **management**
   `user_roles` row for Steve tied to that scratch carrier.
2. As Steve, real session: `is_staff` → **false**, `has_role('management')` → **false**,
   and `settlements`, `brokers`, `loads`, `deductions`, `pay_policies` all returned `[]`.
   `current_company_id()` → his own carrier.
3. Counterfactual: the same role row moved to **his own** carrier → `is_staff` → **true**
   and `settlements` returned a row. So the company predicate is what decides, not
   something incidental.
4. Both scratch rows deleted. After cleanup: 1 carrier, Steve holds `operator` only,
   `rods_days` 2, `rods_events` 0.

The scratch role granted a driver read access to settlements for a few seconds during
step 3. That is the cost of demonstrating the fix rather than asserting it; it was on
reads only and reversed immediately.

Errors met and recorded rather than hidden:

- `SET LOCAL ROLE service_role` and `SET ROLE authenticated` are both refused by the SQL
  runner; the stamp trigger checks `auth.role()`, so the scratch insert was done by
  setting `request.jwt.claims` to `{"role":"service_role"}`.
- Direct `auth.uid()` / `has_role` calls from the SQL runner are refused
  (`permission denied for schema auth`, `permission denied for function has_role`). All
  role-scope evidence above is REST-session evidence for that reason.
